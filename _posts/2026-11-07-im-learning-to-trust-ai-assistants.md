I’m usually cautious about giving AI access to my life. Trying a chatbot is one thing. Connecting it to my email and calendar feels different. Those accounts contain real conversations, commitments, and things I don’t want a tool getting wrong.

But I’ve started connecting them, and I’m finding the assistants useful enough that my attitude is changing.

Right now, I use several tools for different jobs. One is my personal assistant. Another handles research and briefings. I use coding assistants, alongside a coding tool I’ve set up on my personal laptop. There’s also a separate assistant I use at work.

Individually, they work pretty well. Getting them to work together is the part I haven’t figured out.

## The small things made me trust them

My personal assistant checks my email, helps me decide which messages need attention first, reminds me about things, and helps organize my calendar. When I need to find an available slot, I can ask instead of looking through the calendar myself.

It can also alert me when my commute changes. That sounds small, but it’s the kind of help I actually want: something relevant to my day, arriving while I can still do something about it.

These aren’t particularly exciting demos. They’re useful because they take care of ordinary things I would otherwise have to remember or check myself.

The research assistant does a different job. It gathers information and puts together briefings. The coding assistants help with work where I can inspect the output, run it, and decide whether it does what I intended.

My comfort level depends on the task. Trusting an assistant to gather research doesn’t mean I’m ready to let it act on everything it finds.

I’ve had to learn more about connecting accounts and what access I’m giving each tool. I wouldn’t claim I’ve solved the security questions. I’m still cautious. But using them for real tasks has made me more comfortable letting them help.

## I’m still carrying the context between them

Connecting an assistant to my calendar is different from connecting all my assistants to each other.

Each one has its own conversations and context. The assistant helping with my schedule doesn’t necessarily know what I’ve been researching. My coding assistant doesn’t automatically know why I made a decision in another conversation.

I end up copying things across, explaining the background again, or remembering which assistant knows what. The tools save me work, but coordinating them becomes another job.

I’ve started experimenting with giving one assistant a chief-of-staff role. I talk to it, and it delegates specialized work to other agents. That feels closer to what I want. I don’t want to manage a separate relationship with every tool just to get something done.

But that only solves part of the problem. It doesn’t automatically connect the different platforms I use. And I’m not sure sharing everything would be a good idea anyway, especially across personal and work tools.

So that’s where I am. I’ve found enough everyday value to start trusting these assistants with more of my work. I still want to understand their limits and check what matters.

Now I’m trying to figure out how to make them work together without having to explain my life to each one, and without giving every assistant access to all of it.
